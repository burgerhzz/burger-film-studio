burgerSub

Taiwanese subtitle generation

How it used to work

Subtitles used to mean running the audio through CapCut, converting the Simplified Chinese output to Traditional, then importing the SRT into Premiere. Taiwanese expressions were often misheard (欸 came out as 哎), and any change in accent, pace or background noise meant more errors to fix by hand at every step.

What it changes

  • Never leave PremiereIt reads the audio straight from the timeline. If an hour of footage is cut down to three minutes, only those three minutes get transcribed.
  • No more conversionA speech model trained for Taiwanese Mandarin outputs Traditional Chinese directly, and 欸 is no longer heard as 哎.
  • Fix a word onceTerms corrected in the editor are remembered and fed to the model on the next run.
  • Line breaks that make senseA Traditional Chinese word-segmentation model breaks lines at phrase boundaries instead of at a fixed character count.
  • Align to a scriptIf there's already a script, the text is aligned to the timeline. Lines that weren't spoken turn grey and don't throw off the timing after them.

Core features

  • Speech recognition
  • Script alignment
  • Custom dictionary
  • Transcribes only the parts in use
  • In/Out range
  • Subtitle editor: split, merge, retime, find & replace
  • Standalone Mac app: burgerSub Studio

Version 1.0 released October 2026. Runs entirely on your Mac: audio never leaves the computer and there are no usage fees.

Decisions behind it

Every line-break threshold was measured against real show subtitles, not picked at random. Loosening a threshold to rescue a few more cases usually lets the wrong ones through too, so every change is checked with a before-and-after comparison across the whole subtitle library.
